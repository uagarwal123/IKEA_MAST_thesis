A resident cycle is a sequence of distinct residents $r_{1}, \ldots, r_{n}$ such that for each $1 \leq i \leq n-1$, the residents $r_{i}$ and $r_{i+1}$ know how to make a common dish, residents $r_{n}$ and $r_{1}$ know how to make a common dish, and no other pair of residents $r_{i}$ and $r_{j}, 1 \leq i, j \leq n$ know how to make a common dish. Two resident cycles are indistinguishable if they contain the same residents (in any order), and distinguishable otherwise. For example, if $r_{1}, r_{2}, r_{3}, r_{4}$ is a resident cycle, then $r_{2}, r_{1}, r_{4}, r_{3}$ and $r_{3}, r_{2}, r_{1}, r_{4}$ are indistinguishable resident cycles.
Compute the number of distinguishable resident cycles of length 6 in $\mathcal{F}_{8}$.

**Key Idea:**  
This is a combinatorial problem about counting certain cycles in a special graph.  
We are told that in a full town $\mathcal{F}_d$, every pair of $d$ dishes is known by exactly one resident, and each resident knows exactly two dishes. Thus, the residents correspond to the $\binom{d}{2}$ unordered pairs of dishes.  
We are to count the number of distinguishable resident cycles of length 6 in $\mathcal{F}_8$.

**Analysis:**  
- Residents: Each is a pair of dishes, so there are $\binom{8}{2} = 28$ residents.
- Two residents share a dish if and only if their pairs overlap in one dish.
- The graph of residents is the line graph of the complete graph $K_8$.
- A resident cycle of length 6 is a cycle of length 6 in this line graph, with the property that no two non-consecutive residents in the cycle share a dish (i.e., their pairs are disjoint).

**Restate:**  
Count the number of distinguishable cycles of length 6 in the line graph of $K_8$, such that consecutive pairs share a dish, and non-consecutive pairs are disjoint.

But in the line graph of $K_8$, a cycle of length 6 corresponds to a cycle of length 6 in $K_8$ (since the edges of a 6-cycle in $K_8$ correspond to 6 residents, each knowing the two dishes at the ends of the edge).

Thus, the number of distinguishable resident cycles of length 6 in $\mathcal{F}_8$ is the number of cycles of length 6 in $K_8$, up to cyclic order and direction.

**So, the problem reduces to:**  
How many distinguishable cycles of length 6 are there in $K_8$?

from sympy import binomial

# Number of ways to choose 6 vertices from 8
choose_6 = binomial(8, 6)

# Number of distinct cycles on 6 labeled vertices: (6-1)!/2
# (since cycles are counted up to rotation and reversal)

# Total number of distinguishable cycles
